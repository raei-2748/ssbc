2027 Sydney Interschool Business Case Competition

A company that unfolds in real time, not a report due in three weeks.

Teams of three to four students take over a fictional Australian infrastructure operator and steer it through four live episodes — no finance background assumed, no single right answer. Every team receives the same information at the same time.

Four episodes, one continuous company

Every round changes the same company your team has been running since episode one — decisions compound, and there's no reset button between episodes.

  1. 01
    Episode one
    Growth
    Fund and price an expansion into a live infrastructure market. Teams choose a growth strategy and defend the numbers behind it.
  2. 02
    Episode two
    Competition
    A rival operator enters the market. Teams respond with pricing, positioning, or a partnership — and live with the consequences in later episodes.
  3. 03
    Episode three
    Shock
    An energy or regulatory shock tests the plan under pressure. This episode rewards teams that built in a margin of safety.
  4. 04
    Episode four · Live final
    Financing
    A debt-versus-equity call, closed out with a recorded pitch to a professional judging panel at the live final.

How judging works

Every episode is scored against the same four criteria, so teams know exactly what's being evaluated from day one.

01

Strategic reasoning

Is the decision well-argued from the information given, not just confidently stated?

02

Financial rigor

Do the numbers hold up — revenue, costs, and financing choices that actually work together?

03

Communication

Can the team explain a complex call clearly to a non-specialist judge?

04

Adaptability

How well does the team respond when new information changes the picture mid-episode?

Format

Two rounds: an online qualifier, then a live final.

Round 1 runs entirely online across all four episodes, giving every member school an equal shot regardless of location. For each episode, teams submit a short decision worksheet and a brief written rationale (roughly 150–250 words). Episode four adds a recorded pitch of two to three minutes.

The top teams from Round 1 are invited to the live final — an in-person day where finalists present their episode four decision to a professional judging panel and take questions on the spot.

  • Round 1 — Online
    All four episodes, submitted asynchronously by each team.
  • Live final — In person
    Finalists pitch episode four to an industry judging panel.

Frequently asked

The questions teachers and students ask us most before registering.

Do students need a finance background to enter?

No. Each episode includes everything a team needs to make its decision — the competition is designed so a first-time team and a team with prior finance exposure are working from the same brief.

How are teams formed?

Teams of three to four students are put together by their school's business society. Mixed-year teams are welcome. We are proposing a cap of three teams per school so more schools get a place; this will be confirmed in the participant handbook.

What does a school need to provide?

A staff supervisor to liaise with SSBC and confirm results, and a bit of time from each team between episodes. Round 1 can be run inside or outside class time — schools choose what fits their calendar.

Can students use AI?

Limited, disclosed use is allowed for research and editing; teams declare it with each submission and cannot outsource their substantive analysis. See the rules for the full policy.

Is there a cost to enter?

Entry costs are kept low and confirmed at registration — reach out via Get Involved and we'll walk you through it.

What happens at the live final?

Finalist teams present their episode four financing decision to a panel of industry judges and answer follow-up questions, in the same spirit as a real board pitch.
